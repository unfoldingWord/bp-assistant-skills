# writing-foreground

## Purpose
Identify words or phrases that draw the listener's attention to something the speaker is about to say or do.

## Definition
Foregrounding is a discourse feature in which the speaker uses a marker such as "behold" to focus the listeners' attention on the upcoming information. This is not primarily a figurative expression; it is an attention-directing signal that helps readers notice what follows.

These markers often introduce:
- A new revelation or important announcement
- A statement that should be noticed carefully
- An event, instruction, or truth that the speaker wants the audience to pay attention to

The note should remind translators that their language may have a comparable expression.

## Recognition Patterns

### Hebrew Markers
- **hinneh** ("behold/look") - common attention marker
- **we'attah** ("and now") - common attention marker
- Expressions of sudden notice or emphasis

### Greek Markers
- **idou** ("behold/look") - common attention marker in narrative and discourse

### Common English Patterns
- "Behold"
- "Look"
- "See"
- "Listen"
- "And now"

## Examples from Published Notes

> "Behold, I am with you" (Genesis 28:15)
- The speaker uses "behold" to draw attention to the promise that follows.

> "Behold, your king is coming to you" (Matthew 21:5)
- The marker focuses attention on the important announcement that follows.

## NOT This Issue

| Use Instead | When |
|-------------|------|
| writing-newevent | Introducing a new event or episode rather than drawing attention to what follows |
| writing-background | Providing explanatory or contextual information rather than signaling focus |
| figs-explicit | Adding information that is not explicitly stated in the text |

## Special Case: "Behold me" (hineni)

The expression "behold me" has different interpretations based on context:

| Context | Issue Type | Example |
|---------|------------|---------|
| Inferior expressing availability to superior | **writing-politeness** | Samuel responding to Eli (1 Sam. 3:4) |
| Yahweh announcing action to punish/bless | **figs-idiom** | "Behold me rebuking" = "Now I am about to rebuke" |
| Speaker calling attention to what follows | **writing-foreground** | "Behold" foregrounds information |

## Recognition Process

```
Is the speaker drawing attention to the next statement or action?
  |
  +-- YES: Is the marker mainly focusing attention rather than adding new content?
  |         |
  |         +-- YES --> writing-foreground
  |         +-- NO --> Consider writing-newevent or another issue
  |
  +-- NO --> Not a foregrounding issue
```

## Common Note Patterns

1. **Attention marker**: "The speaker is using the term **behold** to focus the listeners' attention on what he is about to say. Your language may have a comparable expression that you can use in your translation."

2. **Emphasis marker**: "This marker draws attention to the important information that follows. A comparable expression in your language may be more natural."

3. **Narrative focus**: "The writer uses this expression to signal that the reader should pay special attention to the statement that follows."

## Languages Mark Foregrounding Differently

Foregrounding may be marked by:
- Special discourse particles
- Interjections or attention words
- Intonation or pauses in oral languages
- Word order changes

The note should encourage translators to use whatever method is natural in their language for calling attention to important information.
