# writing-quotations

## Purpose
Identify where quote margins (speech tags like "he said," "saying," "answered and said") may need translation guidance.

## Definition
A **quote margin** (also called a speech tag or speech identifier) is the part of a sentence that tells who spoke, whom they spoke to, and introduces what they said. The actual words spoken are the quotation.

| Position | Example |
|----------|---------|
| Before | **She said**, "The food is ready." |
| After | "The food is ready," **she said**. |
| Middle | "The food is ready," **she said**, "Come and eat." |

Key characteristics:
- Quote margins can appear before, after, or in the middle of a quotation
- Some languages use multiple verbs for "said" (e.g., "answered and said")
- Hebrew/Greek patterns may not translate naturally into other languages
- The position and wording of quote margins varies by language

---

## When to Use writing-quotations

Use this issue when:
1. The text has **"answered and said"** or similar double-verb patterns
2. Direct speech occurs **without any quote margin** (no "he said")

---

## Confirmed Classifications from Issues Resolved

| Pattern | Decision | Notes |
|---------|----------|-------|
| "answered and said" | writing-quotations | "and said" is quote margin like "saying" |
| Direct speech without quote margin | writing-quotations | ULT uses quotation marks; suggest adding speech identifier |

### Special Case: "Answered and Said"

This common Hebrew construction requires writing-quotations:

- **"and said"** is viewed as a quote margin (speech tag) comparable to "saying"
- Put discussion in **book intro**; first instance refers back to intro
- First instances in subsequent chapters refer back to first instance or book intro

**When "answered" responds to a situation** (not to something someone said):
- Still use **writing-quotations** for "and said"
- Write a separate **figs-explicit** note for the situation response
- Example: "The elders are responding to a situation, not something that someone said here."

## Common Patterns Requiring writing-quotations Notes

### 1. "Answered and Said"

| Example | Note Approach |
|---------|---------------|
| "answered and said to him" | May combine to "answered" or "replied" |
| "answered and said" (responding to situation) | Separate figs-explicit for situation |

### 2. Missing Quote Margins

| Example | Note Approach |
|---------|---------------|
| Direct speech without "he said" | Suggest adding speech identifier |
| Dialogue without clear speaker | Help identify who is speaking |

## Recognition Process

1. **Identify quote margins in the text**:
   - Look for speech verbs (said, answered, commanded, asked, replied, etc.)
   - Check for missing margins (direct speech without introduction)

2. **Determine the pattern**:

   | Pattern | Action |
   |---------|--------|
   | "answered and said" | Note about combining verbs |
   | No quote margin present | Note about adding identifier |
